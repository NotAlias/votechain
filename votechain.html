No ticket? Make your own voter key

This creates a key in your browser that nobody else has. You can vote with it straight away. The ticket is secret: it is what lets you vote, so save it. The public key is what you give the organiser if they need to confirm who you are.

Check my vote

Paste your receipt (or load the receipt file) to see exactly how your vote was counted. A ticket, public key or ballot ID shows whether your ballot is on the list of counted ballots. Everything is checked locally against the chain file you loaded.

Call a new election

Each position has its own nominees and its own count. Positions, nominees and the number to elect are fixed once the election is created.

Every ballot is counted in exactly one pool, and results are published for each pool and for all pools together. An open pool accepts a ballot from any key, with no ticket needed. After someone votes there you can confirm who they are and admit their key to another pool. For the other pools you can generate tickets to hand out, paste public keys voters sent you, or admit keys later.

Run the election

Both actions below need your organiser key and act on the chain loaded in the Chain file tab. Merge every file you have received first.

Admit voters to a pool

For people who voted in the open pool (or will vote with their own key) and whom you have confirmed. One per line: their public key, then optionally their name. The key and name become part of the public record; how they voted does not.

Close the election and publish the count

Closing decrypts the ballots on this computer and writes a final block containing the list of anonymous ballots and the result. It does not reveal which key cast which ballot, and your key stays secret. This cannot be undone.
